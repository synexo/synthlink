SynthLink

A BBS terminal that dials over a real software modem.

Nothing but modulated audio crosses the wire. Keystrokes become PCM tones in the browser, travel to the server as sound, and are demodulated by a software modem before reaching the BBS — and the BBS's output makes the same trip back. The carrier you hear is the actual data.

The modems

Bell 103 and V.21 at 300 bps, V.22 at 1200, V.23 at 1200/75, V.22bis at 2400, V.29 and V.32 at 9600, V.32bis at 14400, V.34 up to 33600, and V.90 at 56k. Pick one from the speed menu beside the BBS list before connecting; the slow ones are worth hearing once. V.34 at 33600 is the default.

V.90 is the odd one out, and authentically so. Real 56k modems were never symmetric: the exchange sent you digital PCM samples straight off the phone network, and you answered with an ordinary V.34 carrier. So it runs 56000 down and 33600 up, and the fast direction is not modulated at all — the audio you hear downstream is the data. It sounds like noise rather than a carrier, which is exactly right.

The last entry, Telnet · modem bypass, skips the modem altogether and connects at the speed of your network. There is no carrier to hear, so the scope becomes a throughput graph for the call.

Using it

  • Choose a BBS, or click the pencil to enter a host:port.
  • On a phone, touch the terminal for the keyboard, again to magnify.
  • The handset icon picks up an extension on the line — disrupting the carrier, exactly as it did in 1994.
  • The share icon copies a link to whatever board and speed you have selected. Opening that link dials straight in.

Linking to a board

A SynthLink address can name where to call and how:

?host=bbs.example.org&port=23&speed=v32bis&connect=1

Only host is required — the port falls back to 23 and the speed to V.34 33600. Speeds are named by protocol rather than by bit rate, because the rates collide: 300 is both V.21 and Bell 103, and 9600 is both V.29 and V.32. Use v21, bell103, v22, v22bis, v23, v29, v32, v32bis, v34, v90 or telnet; v34 means its top rate, and v34-28800 picks a slower one. connect=1 greets the visitor with a Connect button rather than dialling by itself; drop it to land on a terminal that is simply pointed at the board.

A link like this only sets up that visit — it never changes the settings you have saved here.
